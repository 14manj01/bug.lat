How to become an Army entomologist

A sourced guide to the U.S. Army medical entomologist career (AOC 72B): what the job is, the degree and commissioning requirements, and how the career progresses. Written by a serving Army entomologist.

Requirements FAQ
Disclaimer. Views are my own and do not reflect the U.S. Army or Department of Defense. This page is general information, not recruiting guidance; requirements change, so confirm everything with an Army recruiter and the official sources linked below.

What an Army entomologist does

An Army entomologist is a commissioned officer in the Army Medical Service Corps who holds area of concentration (AOC) 72B, Entomology. The Army's Medical Service Corps career guidance places 72B in Medical Functional Area 72, Preventive Medicine Sciences, alongside nuclear medical science (72A), audiology (72C), and environmental science (72D).

That same guidance describes the duties: the 72B officer "plans, leads, manages, advises, directs, and participates in operational, consultative, training, product development and research in medical entomology to ensure effective control of pests and vectors of disease affecting the health, morale, and environment of all Army personnel," and also serves in command and staff roles that keep property and materiel from being damaged by pests. GoArmy's Entomologist (72B) page summarizes it as providing expertise on the threats insects pose to operations and studying their impact on the health, morale, and operational environment of Soldiers.

In plain terms, an Army medical entomologist protects Soldiers from diseases carried by mosquitoes, ticks, and other arthropods, through surveillance, identification, control programs, and advice to commanders.

Army entomologist requirements

Degree. The Medical Service Corps guidance states that AOC 72B "requires a master's or doctoral degree from an accredited program acceptable to TSG [The Surgeon General] in medical entomology." It lists additional considerations:

  • an M.S. or Ph.D. in a related biological discipline with significant medical entomology coursework and laboratory work;
  • extensive professional entomological experience combined with a graduate degree; or
  • a B.S. in entomology combined with appropriate medical entomology coursework and/or applicable professional experience.

Basic eligibility. GoArmy's 72B page lists being a U.S. citizen by the time you commission, an age range of 21 to 42, an advanced degree, eligibility for a Secret security clearance, being medically and physically fit, and good moral standing. GoArmy's job page also notes that everyone must pass the Army Fitness Test after joining and every year of service.

Certification. For retention at all grades, the same guidance requires 72B officers to keep current DoD Pest Management Certification, renewed through approved DoD courses.

Paths into the Medical Service Corps

All Army officers commission through a handful of routes. GoArmy's Army Officers page lists Army ROTC, the U.S. Military Academy at West Point, Officer Candidate School, and direct commission, which "appoints civilians with professional experience directly to an Officer rank."

  • Direct commission into Army Medicine. For a civilian who already holds the graduate degree, the Army Medical Department (AMEDD) route is the most direct. GoArmy's Army Medicine page lists the Medical Service Corps as one of the six corps of Army Medicine and notes that most AMEDD officers serve three to four years of active duty "through programs like HPSP or direct commission." Talk to an AMEDD recruiter about current openings.
  • ROTC, West Point, or OCS. These are general commissioning routes for officers of every branch (GoArmy). Because AOC 72B requires the graduate-level qualifications above, a bachelor's-level commission does not by itself make someone a 72B.
  • HPSP is not an entomology scholarship. GoArmy's medical scholarships page says the Health Professions Scholarship Program covers medical (including osteopathy), dental, veterinary, nursing, counseling and clinical psychology, and optometry degrees. Entomology is not listed.

After commissioning, new AMEDD officers attend the AMEDD Basic Officer Leadership Course. GoArmy's Army Medicine FAQ says the active-duty course is held at the AMEDD Center & School at Fort Sam Houston and lasts 10 to 14 weeks; Army Reserve officers attend for two weeks.

Day-to-day work and career progression

According to the Medical Service Corps guidance, 72B officers fill positions including:

  • entomologist in the Preventive Medicine Service of an Army hospital or medical center;
  • entomology staff officer in a deployable medical brigade or command, or commander or executive officer of a deployable detachment;
  • staff officer, division chief, or commander within the Army Public Health Center and its subordinate commands;
  • branch chief or instructor at the Medical Center of Excellence;
  • research investigator or department chief at the Walter Reed Army Institute of Research or the U.S. Army Medical Research Institute of Infectious Diseases; and
  • director or liaison officer at the Armed Forces Pest Management Board.

The guidance also lists skill milestones by rank: DoD Pest Management Certification as a lieutenant or captain, board certification through the Entomological Society of America as a major, the 9A designator in entomology as a lieutenant colonel, and committee chair on the Armed Forces Pest Management Board as a colonel. A limited number of officers are selected by competitive board to return to graduate school for an M.S. or Ph.D. in entomology.

Like other officers, entomologists also take on leadership roles. GoArmy's officer rank overview notes that a captain commands company-sized units alongside a First Sergeant.

My path to becoming an Army medical entomologist

I am a Captain in the U.S. Army and have served as an Army Entomologist since 2023. My route was graduate school first:

  • Teaching Assistant, Baylor University2018 – 2022
    Taught undergraduate biology courses and lab sections.
  • Research Assistant, Baylor University2021 – 2022
    Mosquito species records across Texas, including the first Texas record of invasive Aedes japonicus, and a CRISPR-Cas12 arbovirus detection method supported by the PrimedSherlock crRNA design tool.
  • Ph.D. in Molecular Biology, Baylor University
    Presented research at the Military Health System Research Symposium (MHSRS), as described in the Baylor Graduate School feature.
  • Army Entomologist, United States Army2023 – present
    Vector-borne disease surveillance and applied ecology; previously a Company Commander leading more than 150 medical soldiers.

The full details are on my Army entomologist résumé, and the peer-reviewed work is listed on my medical entomology publications page.

Army entomologist FAQ

What is the AOC for an Army entomologist?

Army entomologists hold area of concentration (AOC) 72B, Entomology. It sits in Medical Functional Area 72, Preventive Medicine Sciences, within the Army Medical Service Corps.

What degree do you need to be an Army entomologist?

The Army's Medical Service Corps career guidance requires a master's or doctoral degree in medical entomology from an accredited program acceptable to The Surgeon General. It also lists alternatives: a graduate degree in a related biological discipline with significant medical entomology coursework and lab work, extensive professional entomology experience combined with a graduate degree, or a bachelor's degree in entomology combined with appropriate medical entomology coursework or professional experience.

Is an Army entomologist an officer or enlisted job?

It is an officer job. GoArmy lists Entomologist (72B) as an officer position, and the Army's Medical Service Corps guidance awards AOC 72B to commissioned Medical Service Corps officers.

Does the HPSP scholarship cover entomology?

GoArmy describes the Health Professions Scholarship Program as covering medical (including osteopathy), dental, veterinary, nursing, counseling and clinical psychology, and optometry degrees. Entomology is not on that list.

What training do new Army entomologists attend?

New Army Medical Department officers attend the AMEDD Basic Officer Leadership Course, which GoArmy says lasts 10 to 14 weeks for active-duty officers at Fort Sam Houston. Army guidance also requires 72B officers to hold current DoD Pest Management Certification for retention at all grades.
